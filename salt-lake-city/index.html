Water Damage We See Most in Salt Lake City

Not every water problem announces itself with a flooded floor. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around Salt Lake City, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Thermal imaging and moisture readings let us map how far it spread before anything gets cut open. Left alone, constant dampness rots framing and feeds mold, so a minor drip is still worth fixing now.

Sewage: A blocked sewer line sends waste back into the house, usually at the lowest shower, toilet or floor drain. Tree roots, grease buildup and heavy rain overloading the public system are the common triggers. This is Category 3 water, which carries bacteria and should not be handled without protective gear. Porous items it touches, such as carpet and pad, generally have to be removed.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. From there it comes in under doors, through window wells and across garage floors. Floodwater from outside picks up soil, fertilizer and whatever else it crossed, so we treat it as contaminated. Homes across Salt Lake City can see this in any season with a strong enough storm.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Standing water, a torn vapor barrier or a leaking pipe overhead keeps the air below your floors humid. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. Our team removes the water, dries the framing and can point out what is letting moisture in.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. It is meant to exit through a narrow drain tube, and that tube plugs up with slime and debris. When it backs up, the pan overflows into the ceiling, closet or hallway around the air handler. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.

How Long Drying Takes in a Salt Lake City Home

A surface that feels dry is not proof that a room is dry. The face of drywall often seems normal when the cavity side is damp, and carpet fibers dry long before the padding underneath. That is why professional drying runs on instruments. Our Salt Lake City technicians carry probe meters for wood and wallboard, scanning meters that read through finishes, and infrared cameras that pick up damp areas as cooler spots.

Not everything dries at the same rate. Drywall and carpet give up moisture fast, but studs, subflooring, hardwood and concrete are slow. The layout of dehumidifiers and air movers follows those rates and is changed as each day's numbers come in. A span of 3 to 5 days covers most jobs, and we will tell you early if yours looks like an exception.

Stopping early is the costly mistake. Materials closed up while still damp can grow mold, swell, or come loose months later, long after the job seemed finished. So for every Salt Lake City job we dry to a measured target and keep the readings on file.
